# Serials landing page — your launch tasklist

**Page:** `https://onfolio.press/for/serials/`
**Goal:** first ~20 signups from serial-fiction writers in the next two weeks, from concrete distribution moves you can do yourself.

Not a marketing plan. A checklist. Do them in order. Stop and measure after the first block before doing the second.

---

## Block 1 — Do these first (this week, ~2 hours total)

### 1. Confirm the page renders and reads right (5 min)
Open `https://onfolio.press/for/serials/` in an incognito tab, on desktop AND on your phone. Read it out loud. If any line feels off in your voice, tell me and I'll rewrite. If everything scans, move on.

### 2. Send #7 (real testimonials) into motion (30 min)
The serials page has no testimonials block on purpose — a placeholder would undercut it. But the **home page** (`onfolio.press`) still hides its testimonials section because there are 0 approved. That section reads as a trust hole to anyone who arrives from the serials page and clicks "Home."

- Send the outreach template from `docs/TESTIMONIAL_OUTREACH.md` to Thomas and Ken. One-to-one messages, personalised. Not a group email.
- Aim for 2-3 responses in the next 5 days. Approve in `/admin/reviews/` when they land.

### 3. Write the pitch you're going to paste (20 min)
The page speaks to serial writers, but YOU need one paragraph you'll paste into each community. Draft it once, use it many times. Something like:

> Built a publishing tool for serial-fiction writers who want to own their audience — no Wattpad lock-in, no KDP exclusivity, 0% commission on tips and paid unlocks, and bonuses (art, mini-games, translations) that drop alongside episodes. Free to start. Would love feedback: onfolio.press/for/serials

Write yours in your voice. Keep it under 50 words. Don't oversell.

### 4. Pick your first 3 channels (10 min)
Rank by fit + how much you already belong there. Don't post cold to a community you've never contributed to — mods will remove it and the community will remember.

**Highest-fit channels to consider:**
- **Reddit `r/selfpublish`** — pinned "self-promo" days most subs run; check their sidebar rules. Post as a discussion ("does a browser-based publisher change what you'd write?") not an ad.
- **Reddit `r/HFY`** — enormous serial-fiction community. Skew is sci-fi/fantasy, matches your catalog exactly. Their meta threads are the best entry.
- **Reddit `r/WebFiction`** — smaller but on-topic. Post the page directly with a "made this for us, feedback welcome" framing.
- **Royal Road forums** — this is the serial-fiction community. Requires standing (post a chapter of your own work there first). Long-term play.
- **Substack Notes** — indie-writer heavy. Share your own writing journey + link the tool. Never spam.
- **NaNoWriMo forums** — if timing lines up (Nov). Support communities year-round.
- **A serial-fiction Discord** (search "serial fiction discord" — there are several active ones). Introduce yourself first; share after you've been around a week.

Pick **3**. Don't do more. Overposting kills you faster than underposting.

### 5. Post in the first channel (15 min)
Pick the one you're most at home in. Post your pitch + link. Reply to every comment personally within 6 hours. Don't argue if someone pushes back; thank them for the reaction and note it.

## Block 2 — Only after Block 1 is done + you have testimonials landed

### 6. Post in channels 2 and 3, spaced 2-3 days apart
Same pitch, adjusted for the community's vibe. Note which channel converts best.

### 7. Track responses in one place
Create a simple sheet or note with columns: **Where I posted · When · Reception (0-5) · Signups from this source (guess) · Notes**. This is how you'll know which channel to double down on. Don't skip this — pattern will emerge fast.

### 8. Add a shelf-side variant of the CTA
Once the serials page has traction, add a small "Are you a writer? Publish your serial here →" CTA on the Shelf that links to `/for/serials/` for readers who might also be writers. I can do that in code when you say go.

### 9. Watch for the first three signups
When a real new user signs up from one of these channels:
- They get the welcome email automatically (Task #32).
- Reach out personally within 24 hours: "Saw you signed up, welcome, anything I can help with?" Personal-touch retention is your superpower at this scale.
- Ask if they'd share what brought them to Folio. Their answer tells you which of your channels landed.
