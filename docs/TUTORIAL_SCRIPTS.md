# Folio tutorial scripts — for OBS recording

A sequenced series, basic to advanced. Each script is short enough to
record in one take, structured so the presenter can talk naturally
over the actions without reading a wall of prose. Send me the raw
footage after and I'll cut, add captions, punch in b-roll, and
produce the finished tutorial.

Recording notes for all of them are at the bottom.

## Series 01 — First folio in five minutes

**Target duration**: 4-5 minutes. **Learning goal**: someone who
signed up two minutes ago has a published folio at the end.

**Setup**:
- Sign into a fresh Google account (or use a test account) so the
  first-time experience is honest.
- Have a Word doc ready on your desktop with 2-3 chapters and
  chapter headings styled as Heading 1. Any short public-domain
  text works — a chunk of Alice in Wonderland is fine.

**Script**:

1. *(0:00)* "This is Folio. If you have a manuscript, I'll show you
   how to get it published and readable at a link in about four
   minutes. Ready?"
2. *(0:10)* Navigate to `onfolio.press`. Click **Sign in with Google**
   in the top right. Pick the test account.
3. *(0:20)* Land in the editor. "Everything about your book lives on
   this page. On the left is where you build it, on the right you
   see it as your readers will see it."
4. *(0:35)* Click **Manuscript** tab. Point at the Import panel.
   "Drop your Word doc here."
5. *(0:45)* Drag the .docx onto the drop zone. It uploads. Chapter
   detection dropdown says Auto. Chapter list populates.
6. *(1:00)* "It found my three chapters. If it got it wrong I could
   pick a different detection mode, or use Split view to mark
   them by eye — but this looks right, so I'll accept it."
7. *(1:15)* Click **Book** tab. Point at Trade 6x9 in the dropdown.
   "Default is a standard trade paperback size. Change it if you
   want a different feel — Digest is a bit smaller, A4 or Letter
   for a manual."
8. *(1:30)* Cycle through 2-3 body fonts. "Pick a font that fits
   the mood."
9. *(1:45)* Add a book title + author name if not already in the
   doc's front matter.
10. *(2:00)* Click **Folio** tab (cloud icon). Point at the folio
    name field. Type a name. Click **Save**.
11. *(2:15)* Click **Release / Manage** (or whatever the primary
    release CTA is called). Modal opens. Toggle "Publish". Toggle
    "List on Shelf" (leaving off keeps it private-link-only).
12. *(2:30)* Click **Publish**. Toast confirms. Modal shows the
    reader URL.
13. *(2:45)* Copy the URL. Open in a new tab. "Here's what your
    readers see."
14. *(3:00)* Scroll a page or two. Show the paginated reader,
    header, footer, page number.
15. *(3:15)* Back in the editor: click **📊 Metrics** tab.
    "Any views, subscribers, or reviews will appear here. Refresh
    to update the numbers."
16. *(3:30)* "That's it. You have a folio, it's published, it's
    live at a link, and you know how to check on it. If you want
    to charge for it, add audio, or run a serial release,
    watch the next tutorials in this series."

**Post-production notes**:
- Cut the sign-in flow to under 5 seconds — nobody wants to watch
  OAuth.
- Punch in on the reader URL when it's copied so the URL is
  legible.
- Overlay a text card at 2:15 flagging "You keep 100% of tips and
  paid-release revenue. No commission."

## Series 02 — Design a folio that actually looks good

**Target duration**: 5-6 minutes. **Learning goal**: an author who
has imported a manuscript makes deliberate design choices instead of
accepting defaults.

**Setup**:
- Start with a folio already imported (skip the import — that's
  Series 01). Ideally use a real folio-in-progress so the
  demonstration lands with real content.

**Script**:

1. *(0:00)* "Defaults are fine, but a couple of small choices
   turn a fine folio into one that feels like a book you'd want to
   own. Let me show you the ones that matter most."
2. *(0:15)* Click **Book** tab. Show the page-size dropdown. "Trade
   6x9 is your safe default. Pocket if you want something intimate.
   Custom if you're doing something unusual — a chapbook, a zine."
3. *(0:45)* Toggle drop caps on. Point at the first paragraph of
   Chapter 1. "One little choice, big difference."
4. *(1:00)* Change body font. Cycle through three options. Pause
   on the one you like. "I read for a living and I still can't tell
   you why one serif reads more comfortably than another. Just
   pick the one that feels right when you read the first page."
5. *(1:30)* Change line spacing from 1.5 to 1.7. "Loose spacing is
   easier on the eye. Tight is more literary. Split the difference
   unless you know what you want."
6. *(2:00)* Justification toggle. Show justified vs left-aligned
   side by side by toggling. "Justified is book-standard. Left-
   aligned is more casual and slightly easier on some readers."
7. *(2:30)* Click **Images** panel. Upload a cover image. Show it
   in the preview.
8. *(3:00)* Turn on chapter images. Show how it cycles per chapter.
   "One image per chapter, cycles through your uploads. Skip it if
   you don't have images — it looks great without."
9. *(3:20)* Ornament dropdown. Cycle a few. "Scene breaks use this
   — pick one that fits the tone."
10. *(3:45)* Running header mode. Show 'both' vs 'title only'.
    "Both means author on the left page, book title on the right —
    the classic book layout."
11. *(4:15)* Set a dedication in Book -> Details. Show how it
    renders on its own page in the preview.
12. *(4:45)* Save. Refresh preview. "Now it looks like a book."
13. *(5:00)* "That's design. Next up: publishing so readers can
    actually get to it."

**Post-production**:
- Split-screen or before/after comparisons work great here — show
  the same page with default vs. thoughtful choices.
- Slow the drop-caps toggle down; that's a visual "ah-ha" moment.

## Series 03 — Publish, share, and go serial

**Target duration**: 5-6 minutes. **Learning goal**: an author knows
the difference between private-link, Shelf, and serial releases, and
picks the right one for their situation.

**Setup**:
- One folio ready to publish. Keep the release modal closed at start.

**Script**:

1. *(0:00)* "Three ways to publish, three different audiences. Let's
   talk through them."
2. *(0:15)* Open the release modal. Point at "Publish" toggle.
   "This makes your folio readable at the link. Off means only you
   can see it — you can share it with editors and beta readers
   using specific roles instead."
3. *(0:40)* Point at "List on Shelf". "This adds you to Folio's
   public catalog. Off means the link works, but nobody browsing
   the Shelf will see it. Good for a personal-audience-only folio;
   turn it on when you're ready to be discovered."
4. *(1:15)* Point at "Adult content". "If your work contains adult
   material, tick this. Adult folios get flagged appropriately and
   aren't shown to logged-out browsers."
5. *(1:40)* Skip to the Serial section of the modal. Toggle "Serial
   release" on.
6. *(1:55)* Show the schedule picker. "One chapter every day, week,
   or fortnight — you pick. Existing subscribers get an email when
   each chapter drops."
7. *(2:20)* Set a schedule (e.g. 3 chapters per week starting
   tomorrow).
8. *(2:35)* Point at "Teasers". "These are chapters that stay
   publicly readable even before your official cadence releases
   them — great for pulling in new readers on social media. I'm
   going to mark chapter 1 as a teaser."
9. *(3:00)* Save release. Modal closes. Toast confirms.
10. *(3:15)* Copy the reader URL. Open in incognito. Scroll to
    show what a first-time reader sees — including which chapters
    are locked (with a lock icon and unlock date) and which are
    teaser-open.
11. *(3:45)* Back in the editor. Click **Manage** on the release
    banner. Turn Serial off. Save. "You can flip serial off any
    time — everything unlocks immediately."
12. *(4:00)* Turn Serial back on. Show the shelf-listing toggle
    once more.
13. *(4:15)* Click your author avatar / imprint. Land on the
    imprint page. Point at the customize button. "Everything about
    your author profile lives here — bio, imprint colours, folios
    on display. Free tier gets the basics; Indie unlocks custom
    styling."
14. *(4:45)* "That's publishing. Serial for building anticipation,
    Shelf for discovery, private link for the people you choose.
    Charging for it? Next tutorial."

**Post-production**:
- Insert a visual "3 paths" diagram at 0:15 that lights up each
  option as you talk about it.
- Cut the actual save/toast confirmations if they take longer than
  a second.

## Series 04 — Getting paid: tips, paid releases, and boosts

**Target duration**: 5-6 minutes. **Learning goal**: an author knows
which monetisation option fits which situation, how commission works,
and how to set up each.

**Setup**:
- Folio published from Series 03. Bring up the release modal fresh.

**Script**:

1. *(0:00)* "Three ways to make money on Folio: tips, paid releases,
   and Featured Boosts you buy to promote your own folio. Folio
   takes 0% commission on the first two. You keep everything."
2. *(0:20)* Show the "Tip the author" button in the reader footer.
   "Readers hit this, drop you a couple of dollars, done. Free tier
   has this. No setup — money goes straight through PayPal or
   Gumroad to you."
3. *(0:50)* Back in the editor. Open the release modal. Scroll to
   pricing. Toggle "Paid release". Show the Gumroad link field.
4. *(1:15)* "Paid releases work through Gumroad — you set up the
   product there, paste the license URL here, readers pay Gumroad,
   Gumroad pays you. Folio just handles the unlock." (Show the
   help link in the modal.)
5. *(1:45)* Point at "Free preview chapters". Set to 3. "First
   three chapters stay free-to-read. Chapter 4 onward requires a
   purchase. This is how you convert."
6. *(2:15)* Save release. Open the reader URL in incognito. Scroll
   past chapter 3. Show the paywall lock + purchase modal.
7. *(2:45)* Close incognito. "That's paid releases. Now the third
   thing — Boosts."
8. *(3:00)* Click your folio in the Shelf. Point at the boost CTA.
   "You can pay to feature your own folio at the top of the Shelf
   for 24 hours, 3 days, or a week. It's like buying an ad, but
   it stays on Folio — no Google, no Meta."
9. *(3:30)* Open the boost checkout modal. Show the tier prices.
   "Indie subscribers get 20% off, Imprint gets 50% off. If you're
   going to boost often, the subscription pays for itself in a
   couple of boosts."
10. *(4:00)* Don't complete the purchase — just show the flow.
    Close the modal.
11. *(4:15)* Bonus: the review-for-boost swap. "Leave a review of
    another author's folio, you get a free 24-hour boost on one of
    yours. Encourages the community, gets you seen — everybody wins."
12. *(4:45)* "That's monetisation. Tips are free money. Paid
    releases are the big win. Boosts are optional discovery. Pick
    what fits your work."

**Post-production**:
- Emphasise the 0% commission line with a text overlay.
- Cut Gumroad setup detail — link to a longer tutorial or a help
  article for that.

## Series 05 — Audio: give your folio a voice

**Target duration**: 4-5 minutes. **Learning goal**: an author
either generates TTS or imports their own recording for a chapter
and knows both flows exist.

**Setup**:
- Folio with 2-3 chapters. Google TTS API key handy (or use
  ElevenLabs). A short MP3 recording ready for the import demo.

**Script**:

1. *(0:00)* "Every chapter of your folio can have audio. Two ways:
   generate it with text-to-speech, or import your own recording."
2. *(0:20)* Click **Audio** tab. Point at the chapter list — one
   row per chapter, all showing a grey circle meaning "no audio".
3. *(0:40)* Click Settings / gear. Add a Google TTS API key. "You
   need your own key — Folio doesn't rent one out. It's like $4
   per million characters, so a whole book is a few dollars."
4. *(1:15)* Close settings. Click the generate button on chapter 1.
   Watch it turn from grey to yellow (generating) to green (ready).
5. *(1:45)* Click play on chapter 1. Let it play for 8 seconds.
   "That's Google's WaveNet voice. Great for drafts, or if you
   don't want to record yourself."
6. *(2:15)* Skip to chapter 2. Click the paperclip / import icon.
7. *(2:30)* Choose the MP3 you prepared. It uploads. Row turns
   green.
8. *(2:45)* Click play on chapter 2. Play the first 8 seconds of
   YOUR recording. "That's my voice. Same button, same UI — Folio
   doesn't care where the audio came from."
9. *(3:15)* Back in the reader. Show the play button in the reader
   header. Play a chapter. Point at the seek bar.
10. *(3:45)* "Readers can listen while they read, or listen instead
    of reading. Some readers only ever consume audio — this doubles
    your reach if you build the recordings."
11. *(4:00)* "Recording tips: quiet room, cheap USB mic beats a
    laptop mic, one chapter per session. If you want, send me the
    raw recording and I can normalise + split into per-chapter
    files for you — that's covered in the audio-production
    tutorial."

**Post-production**:
- Include a "before/after" comparison of a chapter with default
  Google voice vs. a warmer human recording — shows the range.
- Include a short "recording setup" b-roll if we can find or make
  one.

## Series 06 — Multi-language folios

**Target duration**: 4-5 minutes. **Learning goal**: an author
publishes the same folio in two languages and understands the
reader-side language switcher.

**Setup**:
- A folio with 2-3 chapters in one language. A short translation of
  chapter 1 pasted in a scratch doc, ready to demo.

**Script**:

1. *(0:00)* "If you write in more than one language — or have a
   translator — you can publish both under one folio. Same URL,
   same imprint page, reader picks the language."
2. *(0:25)* Click **Manuscript** tab. Point at the primary language
   picker. Set to English.
3. *(0:45)* Add a second language via the +Language button. Pick
   French. "Folio adds a per-chapter language toggle."
4. *(1:15)* Click into chapter 1. Point at the language chips at
   the top of the editor pane. Toggle to French. The editor loads
   an empty French version of chapter 1.
5. *(1:45)* Paste the French translation of chapter 1. Toggle back
   to English — original text reappears. "Each language is a
   completely separate copy of the chapter content; the
   architecture is the same folio, same design, different words."
6. *(2:30)* Save. Publish (if not already). Copy the reader URL.
7. *(2:45)* Open in incognito. Point at the language switcher in
   the reader header. Toggle French. Chapter 1 renders in French.
8. *(3:15)* "Toggle back to English. Same folio, same reader
   experience, both languages available."
9. *(3:30)* Back in the editor. Toggle to French, chapter 2.
   "You don't have to translate everything at once — untranslated
   chapters stay in the original language for that reader. Ship
   one chapter's translation, keep going in your own time."
10. *(4:00)* Show the shelf listing. "The Shelf tags multi-language
    folios so browsers can filter by the language they read in."

**Post-production**:
- Overlay language codes visibly on the reader as you switch.
- Callout the fact that Folio doesn't auto-translate — you provide
  the translations. (An AI-translate feature is on the roadmap.)

## Series 07 — Grow: imprint page, subscribers, reviews

**Target duration**: 5-6 minutes. **Learning goal**: an author knows
how to run a small platform of their own on Folio — building a
reader list, showcasing their catalogue, and gathering reviews.

**Setup**:
- Author account with at least two published folios so the imprint
  page has content to show.

**Script**:

1. *(0:00)* "Folio isn't just an editor. Once you've published
   something, you have a public author profile — an imprint page —
   plus tools for building a reader list, gathering reviews, and
   getting discovered."
2. *(0:25)* Click your avatar in the top right of the editor.
   Navigate to your imprint page.
3. *(0:45)* Point at the folios grid. "Everything you've published
   shows here. Readers can browse your work in one place."
4. *(1:00)* Click **Customize imprint** (owner-only button). Modal
   opens.
5. *(1:15)* Change accent colour. Change font. Upload a hero image.
   Save. "Indie tier unlocks styling. Imprint tier adds custom
   subdomain (coming soon) and a few other perks."
6. *(2:00)* Back in a folio. Open the release modal. Point at
   "Newsletter subscribers". "Any reader can subscribe to be
   notified when you publish updates or new chapters. On serial
   releases this becomes an email per chapter."
7. *(2:30)* Open the reader in incognito. Scroll to the subscribe
   panel at the end. Enter an email. Submit.
8. *(2:50)* Back in the editor. Point at the subscribers count in
   the Metrics tab. "It went up by one."
9. *(3:15)* In the reader, scroll to the review section. "Readers
   who finish your work can leave a rating and a written review.
   Approved reviews show on your Shelf listing."
10. *(3:45)* Submit a review as a test reader. Show the "thanks —
    pending moderation" state.
11. *(4:00)* Back in the editor as owner. Show the notification
    that the review was submitted.
12. *(4:15)* "One more thing: if you leave a review of someone
    else's folio, you get a free 24-hour Featured Boost on one of
    your own. Encourages the community, gets you in front of new
    readers."
13. *(4:45)* "Imprint page, subscribers, reviews, boosts — that's
    how you grow. None of it requires you to leave Folio."

**Post-production**:
- Show the customize drawer as split-screen with the imprint page
  live-updating as changes are made.
- Add a small callout when the subscribers count ticks up.

## Series 08 — Metrics: what the numbers mean and what to do about them

**Target duration**: 4-5 minutes. **Learning goal**: an author reads
their Metrics tab and knows what an underperforming folio looks like
vs. one that's working, plus what to try.

**Setup**:
- Real folio with a few weeks of live data would be ideal (Jacob's
  own Resonance, or Thomas's Psalms of the Heart with permission).

**Script**:

1. *(0:00)* "Metrics are only useful if you know what to do with
   them. Let me walk through what to look at first, what to look
   at last, and what to try when a number is disappointing."
2. *(0:20)* Open a folio. Click **📊 Metrics** tab.
3. *(0:35)* Point at the Views tile. "Total lifetime views. Big
   number, easy dopamine hit, doesn't tell you much on its own.
   You want the trend."
4. *(1:00)* (Indie / Imprint tier) Point at the 30-day sparkline.
   "This is the trend. A rising sparkline means your folio is
   picking up momentum — probably because someone shared it, or
   you posted about it. A flat line means it's discoverable but
   not being actively shared. A dropping line usually means
   yesterday's spike is fading."
5. *(1:45)* Point at the drop-off chart. "This is my favourite
   metric. Each bar is a chapter, height is how many readers
   opened it. If chapter 3 is half the height of chapter 2, you're
   losing readers at chapter 2. That's the chapter to reread and
   sharpen."
6. *(2:30)* Point at Subscribers. "Slower to grow than views but
   ten times more valuable. Every subscriber is someone who wants
   to hear from you again. Aim for a 1-2% subscribe rate — if 100
   people read the folio and 2 subscribed, that's healthy."
7. *(3:00)* Point at Reviews + average rating. "Reviews are your
   discoverability engine. A folio with 5 reviews at 4.2 average
   converts far better than one with no reviews at all — even a
   3.9 average with 5 reviews beats a blank slate."
8. *(3:30)* (Imprint tier) Point at Top countries + Top referrers.
   "These tell you where to invest marketing. If a third of your
   traffic is coming from Reddit, spend more time on Reddit. If
   most is from Australia, that's a real audience you should
   speak to."
9. *(4:15)* "What to do when a number is bad: sparkline flat →
   share the folio somewhere new this week. Drop-off cliff at
   chapter 2 → rewrite the chapter-1 ending. Low reviews → ask
   your 5 most engaged readers directly. Metrics are boring
   until you use them to change something."

**Post-production**:
- Overlay the "1-2% subscribe rate" and "5-review threshold" as
  small info callouts.
- Cut a version of this without the Imprint-tier stuff for Free
  and Indie authors — same script, minus the geo/referrer segment.

## Recording notes (apply to all tutorials)

- **Resolution + audio**: 1080p or 1440p, 30 fps. Audio at 44.1 kHz
  mono. USB headset or lav mic beats a laptop mic every time.
- **OBS scene setup**: browser window taking 80% of screen, no
  taskbar showing. Hide bookmarks bar to keep the frame clean.
- **Cursor**: enable cursor highlighting in OBS (there's a filter
  for it) so viewers can follow the mouse.
- **Talk to a friend, not a camera**: pick one specific person you
  know and imagine you're showing them. Delivery will be warmer.
- **Don't read the script**: use it as a scaffolding. Improvise
  around it. Retakes are cheap.
- **One take per section**: if you fluff a line, keep going and I
  can cut around it. Only start over if you lost the thread.
- **Save the raw file** as `folio-tut-NN-YYYYMMDD-vN.mkv` before
  handing it to me. If there are multiple takes, keep them all —
  I'll pick the best sentences from each.
- **Room**: soft furnishings, closed door, phone on silent. If
  you've got a wardrobe you can record inside, that's actually
  the ideal home vocal booth.

### If both you and Thomas record

Different voices for different tutorials works well — pick who
records which by fit. Rough thoughts:
- Thomas: 01 (First folio), 05 (Audio), 08 (Metrics). Explanatory,
  warm, patient — those are Thomas's strengths.
- Jacob: 02 (Design), 03 (Publish/serial), 04 (Getting paid). More
  design-forward and operational — Jacob's built the mental model.
- Either: 06 (Multi-language), 07 (Grow). Whoever wants it.

Keeping a consistent set of intros and outros across recordings
(same "Hi, I'm ___, welcome to Folio") gives the series a shared
feel even with different presenters.

### Timeline suggestion

- Record 01 first. Ship it as its own thing. See how the
  performance lands before recording the whole series — you may
  want to adjust pacing, length, or tone based on real feedback.
- Then batch-record 02-04 in one session, 05-08 in another.
- Publish weekly to build an audience around the series.
