# Folio intro video — script, prompts, and shot sheet

**Total runtime:** ~75 seconds. Ten shots at 6–8 seconds each. Works with Runway Gen-3, Kling 1.6, Luma Dream Machine, or Sora. Character consistency across shots requires either (a) generating a reference image first and using the model's character-lock feature, or (b) writing the avatar description into every prompt in the exact same words.

---

## The avatar — build once, reuse everywhere

**Character name:** Wren.

**Reference prompt** (generate a still image first, then feed as character reference to the video model):

> *Portrait of Wren, a warm, approachable independent author in their late 20s, ambiguous ethnicity, soft dark curly hair pulled back loosely, wearing a rust-colored knit cardigan over a cream t-shirt, small round glasses, sitting at a warm wooden desk beside a window with golden afternoon light. Slight, genuine smile. Cozy home-office setting with a shelf of hardcover books blurred in the background, a small brass desk lamp, an open notebook. Painterly, natural lighting, soft grain, evocative of a Studio Ghibli / Wes Anderson colour palette — amber, cream, deep green, warm cinematic.*

Keep the cardigan, glasses, and warm desk light constant across every shot. If your generator drifts, re-anchor with the reference image.

## Voiceover script (75 seconds, one continuous narration)

Deliver in a warm, conversational tone. Slight smile in the voice. Not a corporate read — think NPR podcast host, not commercial.

> *(0:00)* You have a story.
>
> *(0:03)* Maybe it's been living in a notebook for years, or in a folder called `chapter-one-final-v7`.
>
> *(0:09)* Folio is where it becomes a book — in your browser, in an afternoon.
>
> *(0:14)* Write, or paste your manuscript in. Add a cover, or pick from the library.
>
> *(0:20)* Chapters, series, sagas — Folio understands the shape of long fiction.
>
> *(0:25)* When you're ready, hit release. Your folio is live at its own URL. Send it to your list, hand it to a reader.
>
> *(0:33)* Or list it on the Shelf — Folio's public marketplace, where readers browse by genre, mood, and language.
>
> *(0:41)* And you're not alone. The Guild connects you with other authors — for newsletter swaps, group promos, and the editors, cover designers, and narrators who help your book happen.
>
> *(0:52)* No commission. No walled garden. The rights, the readers, and the revenue stay with you.
>
> *(1:01)* Folio. Beautiful books in your browser.
>
> *(1:07)* Start one at onfolio.press — free, forever.

## Production notes

**Text on screen.** AI video still can't render specific words reliably. Every UI shot above is written to keep text abstract — actual "chapter one" wording, book titles, button labels are all added in post over solid frames. Use CapCut, DaVinci Resolve, or ClipChamp. This is the single biggest quality lever.

**Voice.** Record yourself, or use ElevenLabs with a warm, mid-range voice (I'd suggest "Rachel" or "Charlotte" from their library, or clone your own). The script is timed for a natural pace — around 145 words per minute. Read it aloud once with a stopwatch before generating; if you land at 60 seconds, trim shots to match. If 80, stretch the guildhall and cover shots.

**Music.** Something warm, acoustic, and unobtrusive. Search Epidemic Sound for "warm folk instrumental" or "cozy piano indie." Avoid anything that swells triumphantly — this video's mood is *inviting*, not *conquering*.

**Character consistency.** If your model (Runway, Kling) supports a character reference image, generate the Wren reference still first and feed it into every Wren shot. If it doesn't, paste the exact reference prompt into every Wren shot's description. Small deviations (a different sweater colour, a different hair length) will read as disorienting.

**If the guildhall shot is too complex for one generation:** split into two shots — a close-up of Wren laughing at the table (5 seconds), then a wider slow orbit of the room without her in frame (4 seconds). Cheaper and easier to get right.

**Cost estimate.** At Runway Gen-3 pricing (~$0.05/second at Standard), 75 seconds = ~$3.75 per full generation. Budget 3–5 generation passes to nail character consistency and shot quality, so ~$15–20 total. Kling is cheaper if you use their web tier.
